# Context — the why behind this trip

A reference document so anyone reading the itinerary understands *who this trip is for* and *what it's actually trying to prove*. This is not a vacation plan. It's a 10-day experiment in whether a life could be lived here.

---

## Who we are

Two adults, two kids. We're a Brazilian family currently living in **Praia do Rosa, Santa Catarina**. During this trip the kids will be **almost 2** and **3**.

We are visiting Italy — specifically the northern Italian lakes, the Dolomites, and South Tyrol — because we are considering moving. The trip is for Raul, mostly. Rina is already half-convinced. The goal is to put Raul inside a version of daily life that's plausibly ours, for ten days, and see how it feels.

## Why we're looking at all

Praia do Rosa has been wonderful, but it has begun to feel limiting for small kids:

- **Weather.** It can rain for a month straight. When it doesn't rain, the wind can keep us inside anyway. We cycle through the same three child-friendly cafés on a loop because there isn't much else.
- **Beach.** Too strong, too cold for the kids. They can't really swim it.
- **Hiking.** Few options that work with toddlers.
- **Stimulation for the girls.** They are curious, mobile, ready for more. We feel like we are not feeding it.

We want somewhere our kids can be outside, exploring, adventurous, *moving*. Where life feels light to live with two small humans. Where we are not locked in the house.

## What we'd be looking for in daily life

This list defines what each day of the trip should *feel like*. If a day doesn't reflect at least three of these, we got something wrong.

- **Going to a local market** — picking out tomatoes, cheese, bread, fruit — beats supermarket grocery runs.
- **Biking together** with the kids in a trailer or seats.
- **Outdoor adventures** — gentle hikes, lakes to swim in, animals to meet, mountains visible.
- **Soulful food** — fresh, local, made by someone who cares. Not chain, not fast.
- **A neighborhood feel** — walkable old town, places we'd return to.
- **Variety in a single day** — not "drive 2 hours to a thing." A morning thing and an afternoon thing, both close.
- **No car.** Trains, buses, gondolas, ferries, bikes, feet, and the occasional taxi.

## Where Merano fits

Merano is the place we've already decided we feel good about. The trip allocates **4 nights there** — more than anywhere else — because it's the only place we need to test the "what if this were home" hypothesis. Everything else (Garda, the Dolomites) is the showcase to convince Raul that the *region* delivers.

Day 5 onwards, the framing shifts: **what would a Tuesday look like if we lived here?** That's why those days lean into the orchard bike path, the Friday market, the Therme as a Wednesday-afternoon thing, the bus up to Tirolo for sunset farm dinner — the texture of routine, not the highlights reel.

## Food, specifically

- **Lunch is our main meal.** Plan it carefully — sit-down, vegetarian-friendly, local.
- **Dinner is light.** Often we grab something to-go (pizza al taglio, panini, gelato, market picnic) and eat it back at the apartment or on a bench outside. **A few "special dinners" during the trip are great** — particularly a vegetarian-forward sit-down dinner in Merano.
- **Rina is vegetarian. Everyone else eats meat.** Restaurants need to credibly serve both.
- **Fresh, local, soulful.** South Tyrol delivers this everywhere — Buschenschanks (farm taverns), village stube, market food.
- **Asian food is part of our normal rotation.** We'd love at least one Asian meal slotted in, particularly in Merano where it's available.
- **Breakfast must be planned, not assumed.** Either we know which bakery we walk to, or we know we're packing it the night before. With a 6 a.m. wake we're often eating before places open.

## The daily rhythm (this drives everything)

| Time | What's happening |
|---|---|
| 06:00 | Kids wake. Long morning window opens. |
| 07:00 – 12:00 | **Anchor activity.** Best energy of the day — main outdoor thing. |
| 12:00 – 13:00 | **Lunch** — the proper meal. |
| 13:00 – 15:00 | **Nap window.** This is when we travel between bases. Trains and buses are timed to fall here. |
| 15:00 – 19:30 | **Second activity.** Lighter than the morning — a swim, a promenade, a market, a playground, sunset somewhere. |
| ~17:30 – 19:00 | Light dinner — grab-to-go or improvised. A few special dinners as exceptions. |
| 20:00 | Kids in bed. |

This rhythm is non-negotiable and the schedule respects it absolutely.

## Practical constraints

- **No rental car.** Public transport throughout — Trenitalia, SAD (South Tyrol bus), Trentino Trasporti, Navigarda ferries, gondolas. Taxis and Uber where needed.
- **Hotel logistics:** never carry bags to an activity. If check-in is at 15:00 and we arrive at 11:00, bags go to reception or to a station's Kipoint storage.
- **Italian car-seat exemption:** taxis and NCC services are legally exempt from car-seat law. Pre-book NCC with seats for longer rides where we want them.

## What this trip is *not* trying to be

- Not a "see Italy" highlights tour. Venice, Florence, Rome are explicitly skipped.
- Not a multi-stop blur. 5 bases over 12 nights — fewer transfers, deeper stays.
- Not a romantic getaway in disguise. Every day is built around what will work for a 2-year-old and a 3-year-old.
- Not optimized for Instagram. Optimized for "would we want this to be Tuesday."

---

*If you are reading this and the itinerary, the question to keep asking is:* **does this Tuesday feel like a Tuesday I want?**
