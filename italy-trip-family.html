Appendix · Good to know

A few practical notes.

Travelers

Two adults, two kids — ages 3 and almost 2. No rental car at any point; everything by train, bus, gondola, ferry, bike, and a few Ubers.

The day rhythm

Up at 6, big anchor activity 7–12, lunch + nap 13–15, second activity 15–20, dinner 20:00. Travel days are timed so the longest leg falls inside the 1–3 nap window. Bag-storage at Bolzano station for the Carezza detour on Day 5.

Bikes & trailers

Pre-book a double child trailer at Sportler Merano and Garda Trentino Bike — both in town centers, both with online booking. Specify "rimorchio doppio per bambini, 2 e 3 anni". Both kids ride together; the trailer reclines for naps.

Italian taxi / Uber + kids

Italian taxis are exempt from car-seat law (Codice della Strada art. 172). In practice, taxis and Uber Black accept kids without seats. For Parco Natura Viva specifically, pre-book an NCC service that provides seats — hotel concierge can arrange.

Tickets to consider

  • Malpensa Express Family Pass — €36, 2 adults + 2 kids airport ↔ Centrale.
  • Südtirol Guest Pass — free with most South Tyrol accommodations; includes regional buses + trains.
  • Seiser Alm Card Gold — gondola + all area buses, discounted with the Guest Pass.
  • Mobilcard Südtirol — 7-day, €38, fallback if Guest Pass not included.

Stroller & kids reality

Every train, gondola and bus on this route is stroller-friendly. Highchairs are standard. Changing tables at major stations and gondola valley stops. Italian and South Tyrolean restaurants welcome small children at any hour.

Weather

Late June – early July: lake areas 27–30 °C highs, alpine areas 5–10 °C cooler. Lake water swim-friendly throughout. Mountain mornings cool — bring one layer for the kids.

Bags

No checked baggage on the outbound flight (Schengen-internal from Lisbon). One soft duffle per adult plus a kids' bag fits the train luggage racks. Bolzano station Kipoint storage for the Carezza detour on Day 5 — ~€5/bag for the morning.

The food rhythm

Lunch is the main meal — sit-down, vegetarian-friendly, often at a malga (alpine hut), Buschenschank (farm tavern), or traditional stube. Dinner is light: pizza al taglio, panini, market picnic, leftovers — eaten on a bench, on a balcony, on the Passer promenade. One "special dinner" built into the trip: the Tirolo Buschenschank sunset (Day 8). Most other days dinner is 17:30–19:00.

Vegetarian in South Tyrol

Strong. Most stube and malgas offer schlutzkrapfen (spinach-ricotta ravioli), spinach + cheese knödel, kasspatzln, polenta with mushrooms, wild herb plates, and apple strudel as defaults. The Asian rotation lives in Merano old town — Sushi Mantra, Yumami, Bali. Pur Südtirol on Sandplatz is the best vegetarian-friendly graze + market in town.

Breakfast logistics

With 6am wake-ups, most cafés don't open until 7:00–7:30. Plan B: buy cornetti + fruit + yogurt the evening before from a panificio or supermarket; eat in the room or pack for the platform. South Tyrol bakeries usually open 6:30. The Friday market in Merano (Day 9) is the breakfast morning everyone will remember.

Markets to anchor on

Friday — Merano Piazza Prader, 8:00–13:00, the big one. Daily — Merano small market by the Wandelhalle. Sunday — Riva small lakefront market. Daily — Bolzano Piazza delle Erbe, 7:00–19:00. Saturday — Castelrotto small farmer's market.

"Could we live here" cues

Each day there are 2–3 moments built in that approximate daily life rather than tourism: a market shop, a neighborhood walk, a sit-down lunch with a vegetarian default, a bus or bike ride that's how locals move. The question on the back of your hand: does this Tuesday feel like a Tuesday I want?
